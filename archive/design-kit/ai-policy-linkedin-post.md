Same country. Same technology. Opposite AI rules.

No Act of Parliament requires a school to have an AI policy. So schools, trusts, colleges and universities have written their own, and they look nothing alike.

We read ten of them, from a Sheffield primary to three universities.

For one learner, AI is:
→ a supervised tool at 10
→ a privilege, granted task by task, at 15 (or not provided at all)
→ chiefly a malpractice risk at 17 in college
→ the default at 19

They agree on principles: keep personal data out of unapproved tools, declare AI use, and keep a human responsible for checking. They split on practice: marking, detectors, which tools, and whether a student may refuse AI. Only one university says they can.

Swipe for the comparison, and for six questions any head, governor or trust leader can put to their own policy this term. If you cannot answer one, that is the gap.
