# Lesson starters, version 2: a fact and three habits for each theme (Instagram carousel, 13 slides)

`slide-01.png` to `slide-13.png`, each 1080×1350 (4:5). Made from `archive/design-kit/ig-resources-facts-slides.html`
(`PW=<playwright dir> node render-benchmark-slides.mjs <this folder> ig-resources-facts-slides.html`). The first version
(the big questions) is kept in `archive/instagram/2026-10-lesson-starters/`.

This version uses the content of our own lesson slide decks (`assets/lessons/2027/*.pdf`): the "Did you know?" slide, the
"three habits" slide and the key takeaway for the five starters that have a deck, one per theme. Each theme's icon is the
backdrop. "AI Relationships?" is left out because it has no deck and its two figures (20% and 58%) have no source on its page.

1. Cover: five lesson starters, five themes
2. Safe, did you know: 1 in 17 young people have been targeted by deepfake image abuse
3. Safe, three habits (Stop, Verify, Report). Takeaway: Think before you post.
4. Smart, did you know: 92% of UK university students use AI for academic work
5. Smart, three habits (Know what it is, Verify, Be specific). Takeaway: Autocomplete, not an oracle.
6. Creative, did you know: creative thinking is one of the top five skills employers want
7. Creative, three habits (Brainstorm with it, Bring yourself, Start do not finish). Takeaway: You are still the author.
8. Responsible, did you know: AI's estimated 2025 carbon footprint is about New York City's
9. Responsible, three habits (Think first, Check yourself first, Ask for transparency). Takeaway: Thoughtfully, not never.
10. Future, did you know: +78m net new jobs by 2030 (170 million created, 92 million displaced)
11. Future, three habits (Lead with human skills, Learn to work with AI, Stay adaptable). Takeaway: Collaborate, and stay human.
12. Slides and a plan, ready to go
13. Try one tomorrow (link in bio)

Sources are the ones printed on each deck's slide; the figures are as the deck states them, so check any you plan to quote
elsewhere against the original report. The deck's own check slide notes that "8 million deepfakes" was a projection, not a
count, so that figure is deliberately not used.
