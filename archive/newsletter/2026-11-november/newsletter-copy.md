[03-free-ai-audit.png]

How much do you rely on AI? Do you check what it tells you? Could you manage without it?

## Two new articles

[06-two-new-articles.png]

**Oracy joins the curriculum.** The Education Secretary has announced that oracy (public speaking, structured debate and attentive listening) will sit alongside reading and writing in England's national curriculum. We think it's a bet on what will still make us valuable in an age of AI: when a machine can draft the essay, the premium shifts to the person who can explain and defend it. [Read the oracy article](https://aiawarenessday.co.uk/timeline/oracy-and-ai-awareness-day/)

**Is the human the new bottleneck?** Anthropic's latest report found that AI sessions inside the company spend roughly 80% of their time waiting for a human, and the junior share of technical hires fell from 27% to 9%. So what does that mean for teachers, pupils and the career advice we give young people? [Read the bottleneck article](https://aiawarenessday.co.uk/timeline/human-bottleneck-ai-workplace/)

## Eight skills

[11-eight-skills.png]

AI can answer almost any question in seconds. So what should young people be learning? We think it's the skills no machine can practise for them: oracy and communication, public speaking, critical thinking, information literacy, conflict resolution, active listening and empathy, independent judgement, and civil discourse.

That's what the National AI Conversation is for: young people across the UK debating the big questions about AI, in their classrooms, their schools and with schools across the country.

Button: **Explore the National Conversation** → https://aiawarenessday.co.uk/national-conversation/

## A debate to try

[10-dessa-classroom-debate.png]

If an AI sounded exactly like a person, would you still keep a human in the loop?

Block (Jack Dorsey's company) has unveiled Dessa, a research voice AI that learned to talk from recordings of real conversation, with no written text at all. Block says it can listen and speak at the same time, laugh mid-sentence and pick up on pauses and sarcasm. These are the company's own claims; there are no independent tests yet. It makes a perfect classroom debate:

- Safe: if a voice on the phone sounds human, how would you check?
- Smart: when should a voice AI act for you, and when should you decide yourself?
- Responsible: should an AI have to tell you it isn't human?
- Future: if AI can chat like us, what conversation skills must stay human?

Which side would your students take? Block's page: [block.xyz/dessa](https://block.xyz/dessa)
