Artificial Intelligence Ai

Computer systems, built and used by people, that do tasks we once thought only humans could do, such as translating text, recognising faces in photos or recommending what to watch next.

Latent Space Ls

The space of numbers inside a model where each input is placed as a point, so similar things, such as ‘cat’ and ‘kitten’, end up close together. Each point is an embedding (Em).

Recursive Self-Improvement Rsi

AI helping to build better AI, which could then build better AI still. The idea dates from 1965; today, AI companies already use their own models to help train and test the next ones.

Superintelligence Si

A hypothetical AI that would far surpass the best human minds in almost every field. Experts disagree on whether, or when, it could arrive.

Artificial General Intelligence Agi

A hypothetical AI able to match or exceed human ability across almost any intellectual task, rather than excelling in one narrow domain.

Machine Learning Ml

A branch of AI where systems learn patterns from data instead of following hand-written rules.

Deep Learning Dl

Machine learning using neural networks with many layers — the approach behind most modern breakthroughs in vision, speech and language.

Neural Network Nn

A model made of layers of connected nodes, loosely inspired by the brain, that learns by adjusting the strength of its connections.

Algorithm Al

A step-by-step set of instructions for solving a problem; in machine learning, the method a system uses to learn from data.

Model Md

What you get after an algorithm has learned from data: the trained program that turns an input, such as a question or a photo, into an output.

Training Tr

The process of feeding data to a model and adjusting its internal settings until its outputs improve.

Inference If

Using a trained model to make predictions or generate output on new inputs — the “running” phase after training.

Parameters Pm

The millions, billions or even trillions of numbers inside a model that it adjusts as it learns, like dials being tuned. A model’s size is often quoted in parameters.

Token Tk

The small chunk of text — a word, part of a word or a symbol — that language models read and write.

Embedding Em

A list of numbers that represents the meaning of text, images or other data, so that similar things sit close together.

Transformer Tf

The design behind today’s large language models, introduced in 2017. Its “attention” lets it weigh up how each word in a passage relates to every other word.

Foundation Model Fm

A large model trained on broad data that can be adapted to many different tasks, rather than built for just one.

Large Language Model Ll

An AI model trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next word (token), which lets it generate replies, summaries and translations – the kind of model behind most chatbots.

Generative AI Ga

AI that creates new content — text, images, audio, video or code — rather than only analysing existing data.

Prompt Pr

The instruction or input you give a generative model to get a response.

Prompt Engineering Pe

The craft of writing and structuring prompts to get more accurate, useful and consistent outputs.

Context Window Cw

The maximum amount of text, measured in tokens, that a model can take into account at once.

Multimodal AI Mm

A model that can take in or produce more than one type of data, such as text, images and audio together.

Diffusion Model Df

A generative model trained on pictures that have had random speckles (‘noise’) added, so it learns to remove the noise. To make a new picture, it starts from pure noise and cleans it up step by step.

Generative Adversarial Network Gn

Two networks in competition — one generates content, the other judges it — until the output looks realistic.

Text-to-Image Ti

AI that makes a picture from a written description, such as ‘a fox reading a book in a library, cartoon style’. The tool fills in many details you did not ask for.

Text-to-Speech Ts

Converting written text into natural-sounding synthetic speech.

Fine-Tuning Ft

Taking a model that has already been trained on lots of general data and training it a bit more on a smaller set of examples, such as medical notes, so it does better at one job.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation Rg

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): a system first looks up relevant documents, such as a school’s policies, and gives them to the model so its answer can draw on them. The answer can still be wrong.

Temperature Te

A setting that controls randomness in output: low is focused and predictable, high is varied and creative.

Chain-of-Thought Ct

Asking a model to write out steps before its final answer, which often improves results. The written steps may not show how the answer was really produced. Compare Reasoning Model (Rm).

Few-Shot Learning Fs

Showing a model a handful of examples in the prompt so it can pick up a new task without retraining.

Reasoning Model Rm

A model trained to spend extra computation “thinking” through a problem before answering, improving maths, coding and logic.

Synthetic Data Sd

Artificially generated data used to train or test models when real data is scarce, sensitive or expensive.

Hallucination Ha

When an AI produces confident-sounding information that is false or made up, such as a book that does not exist. It happens mainly because the model predicts likely-sounding words rather than checking facts.

Deepfake Dk

Realistic AI-made or edited video, audio or images showing people saying or doing things they never did. Sexual fakes of under-18s are illegal; if you see one, tell a trusted adult or use Childline’s Report Remove.

Vibe Coding Vc

Building software by describing what you want in plain language and accepting the code an AI tool writes, often without reading or checking it closely.

Context Engineering Ce

Structuring everything you give an AI, including background documents, rules and examples, rather than relying on a single clever prompt.

Generative Video Gv

AI that creates video clips from a text description, an image or another video.

AI Slop Sp

Low-effort, mass-produced AI text, images and videos that flood social media and search results.

2D Design 2d

Flat designs such as drawings, logos, layouts and laser-cut parts. AI tools can now suggest, generate and refine them.

3D Design & CAD 3d

Designing objects in three dimensions with computer-aided design (CAD) software, ready to model, 3D print or manufacture. AI can generate and improve designs.

Supervised Learning Su

Training a model on labelled examples, where every input comes with the correct answer, such as thousands of photos each tagged ‘cat’ or ‘dog’.

Unsupervised Learning Un

Training a model to find patterns or groups in data that has no labels, such as sorting songs by how similar they sound, without being given the right answers. Contrast with supervised learning.

Regression Rr

Predicting a continuous number, such as a price, a demand level or a temperature.

Classification Cl

Assigning inputs to categories, such as spam or not spam, fraudulent or genuine.

Clustering Cu

Grouping similar items together when no one has said in advance what the groups should be, such as a shop sorting customers by what they tend to buy.

Decision Tree Dt

A model that makes predictions through a flowchart of yes/no questions about the data.

Random Forest Rf

Many decision trees combined, whose collective vote gives a more robust prediction than any single tree.

Gradient Boosting Gb

Building many small models one after another, each fixing the mistakes of the one before, so together they make good predictions from tables of data.

Feature Engineering Fe

Selecting and transforming raw data into inputs (features) that help a model predict well.

Overfitting Of

When a model memorises its training data so closely that it performs poorly on new, unseen data.

Forecasting Fc

Predicting future values — sales, demand, traffic — from historical patterns.

Time Series Tm

Data recorded at regular times, such as daily temperatures or weekly attendance, and the methods for spotting its trends and repeating patterns.

Anomaly Detection Ad

Spotting unusual data points or behaviour, such as fraud, equipment faults or security breaches.

Predictive Analytics Pa

Using historical data and statistical models to estimate what is likely to happen next.

Prescriptive Analytics Ps

Going beyond predicting what will happen to recommending the best action to take.

Recommendation Engine Rc

A system that suggests products, content or actions based on a person’s behaviour and preferences.

Churn Prediction Ch

Identifying which customers are likely to leave, so a business can act before they do.

Sentiment Analysis Sa

Detecting the emotional tone — positive, negative or neutral — in text such as reviews or social posts.

Natural Language Processing Np

Natural language processing (NLP) is the field of AI that builds systems to process, analyse and produce human language, such as translation apps, spellcheckers and voice assistants.

Computer Vision Cv

The field of AI that lets machines interpret images and video, from recognising faces to spotting defects on a production line.

Generative Engine Optimisation Ge

Writing and arranging web content so that AI chatbots and AI search answers are more likely to mention or link to it, like a business trying to appear first in Google results. Also called answer engine optimisation.

AI Agent Ag

A system that uses an AI model to pursue a goal by deciding on and taking actions, not just answering questions.

Agentic AI Ac

AI that can plan, use tools and carry out several steps of a job by itself, with a person checking in only now and then.

Autonomy Au

How much an AI system can decide and do on its own before a human needs to step in.

Planning Pl

An agent breaking a goal down into ordered steps and deciding what to do next.

Memory Me

How an AI tool stores information to use later, either during one task (short-term) or between separate conversations (long-term), such as a chatbot saving your name.

Function Calling Fn

A way for an AI model to ask other software, such as a calculator, a search engine or a booking system, to do a job; the app around the model runs the request. Also called tool use.

ReAct Ra

A pattern where an agent alternates between reasoning about a problem and acting with tools (Reason + Act).

Agent Loop Lp

The cycle an AI agent repeats (look at the situation, choose an action, act, check the result) until the goal is met or a person stops it.

Self-Reflection Sr

An agent reviewing and critiquing its own work, then correcting mistakes before it finishes.

Model Context Protocol Mc

A shared set of rules (MCP) that lets AI assistants connect to other tools and data, such as a calendar or a folder of files, in the same way every time.

Agent-to-Agent (A2A) Aa

Shared rules that let AI agents made by different companies find each other and work together on a task.

Orchestrator Or

A part of an AI system that splits a big task into smaller jobs, hands them to other agents or tools, then puts their results together. Contrast with Workflow Orchestration, which plans a whole business process.

Multi-Agent System Ma

Several specialised agents working together, each handling one part of a larger task.

Agent Swarm Sw

A large group of simple agents coordinating to solve problems collectively, inspired by swarms in nature.

Reinforcement Learning Rl

A way of training a program by trial and error: it gets a reward score for good actions and is adjusted to get more rewards. Chatbots are partly tuned this way, using people’s ratings of their answers.

Computer Use Co

An AI agent that controls a computer by taking screenshots, moving the cursor and typing, so it can use ordinary apps the way a person would.

Browser Agent Br

An agent that navigates websites, fills in forms and completes online tasks on your behalf.

Coding Agent Ca

An AI agent that writes, tests and fixes code across a whole project, with a programmer setting the task and checking the changes before they are used.

Robotic Process Automation Rp

Software ‘bots’ that copy the clicks and typing a person would do, to repeat the same fixed steps, such as copying data from emails into a spreadsheet (RPA).

Intelligent Automation Ia

Combining simple software bots with AI tools that can pull out information and pick between options, so computers can handle jobs that are not just fixed rules, like sorting emails by topic.

Hyperautomation Hy

When an organisation tries to hand as many of its everyday tasks as possible to computers, using several kinds of software and AI together.

Workflow Orchestration Wo

Planning and running the order of steps in a whole process, across different apps and people, such as getting a new pupil’s details from the form to the register. Contrast with Orchestrator.

Trigger Tg

The event that starts an automation — a new email, a form submission, a scheduled time.

Event-Driven Automation En

Automations that start as soon as something happens in another app, such as a new booking, instead of waiting for a set time. Triggers and webhooks are how this usually works.

API Integration Ap

Connecting apps so they pass information and actions to each other automatically, through their APIs: the doors one program opens for another.

Webhook Wh

A message one app sends to another the moment something happens — often used to kick off a workflow.

Low-Code / No-Code Lc

Building apps and automations with visual, drag-and-drop tools and little or no hand-written code.

Intelligent Document Processing Id

Using AI to read in documents such as forms, letters and invoices, sort them by type and pull out key details like names and dates (IDP).

Optical Character Recognition Oc

Converting images of text, such as scans or photos, into machine-readable text (OCR).

Process Mining Mi

Using the records computers keep to see how work really gets done, where it slows down, and which steps could be automated.

Business Rules Engine Be

Software that follows a list of ‘if this, then that’ rules written by people, so the same case always gets the same decision, such as ‘if a book is 7 days late, send a reminder’.

Straight-Through Processing St

When a computer handles a whole job, such as a payment or an order, from start to finish without a person checking it.

Digital Worker Dw

A marketing name for a software bot or AI agent set up to do a fixed set of tasks, described by its sellers as if it were a member of staff.

Chatbot Cb

A program that people can chat with by typing or speaking, from simple scripted helpers to AI assistants that produce replies from patterns in text. If a chatbot ever encourages you to hurt yourself or keep secrets, stop and tell a trusted adult, or call Childline free on 0800 1111.

Virtual Assistant Va

A voice or chat assistant, such as the one on a phone or smart speaker, that carries out tasks like setting reminders or searching. Contrast with Chatbot (mainly conversation) and Copilot (built into one app).

Copilot Cp

An AI assistant built into an app or workflow that works alongside you — suggesting and drafting rather than acting alone.

Responsible AI Rs

Developing and using AI in ways that are safe, fair, transparent and accountable.

AI Ethics Et

Thinking about what is right and wrong in how people build and use AI, from asking permission to use someone’s data to its effects on jobs and society. Contrast with Responsible AI, which puts these ideas into practice.

Bias Bi

When an AI keeps treating some groups of people unfairly, often because the data it learned from was unbalanced.

Fairness Fa

Making sure an AI system’s outcomes don’t unjustly favour or disadvantage particular people or groups.

Explainability Xa

Ways of showing people why an AI system gave a particular answer or decision, such as which information mattered most (XAI, explainable AI). Contrast with Transparency and Mechanistic Interpretability.

Transparency Tn

Being open about when AI is being used, how it works and what data it relies on.

Alignment An

Research and testing that tries to make an AI system’s behaviour match what people intend and value, for example not taking shortcuts that meet the instruction but miss its point.

Guardrails Gr

Rules and filters that keep an AI system’s inputs and outputs within safe, acceptable bounds.

Human-in-the-Loop Hi

Keeping a person involved to review, approve or correct AI decisions before they take effect.

Red Teaming Rt

Deliberately attacking or stress-testing an AI system to uncover vulnerabilities and harmful behaviour.

AI Safety Sf

Research and practice aimed at preventing AI systems from causing accidental or deliberate harm.

Privacy Pv

Keeping people's personal information safe and under their control, including data used to train or run AI. In the UK this is covered by the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, updated by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.

Data Governance Dg

The policies that control how data is collected, stored, accessed and used across an organisation.

AI Regulation Re

Laws and standards that govern AI — such as the EU AI Act, which sets rules according to a system’s level of risk.

Copyright & IP Ip

Questions over who owns AI training data and AI-generated content, and how creators’ rights are protected. In the UK, creators’ work is protected by the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988.

AI Washing Aw

Exaggerating how much a product uses AI to ride the hype, such as labelling basic software “AI-powered”.

Shadow AI Sh

Using AI tools at work or school that haven’t been approved, which can put private data at risk.

Human Premium Hp

The extra value people place on work, art and services verified as made by humans.

AI Watermarking Wm

Signals, often invisible, added to AI-generated images, audio, video or text so tools can later check where they came from. Watermarks can sometimes be removed, so they are not proof on their own.

AI Sovereignty So

A country’s or organisation’s ability to build, run and control its own AI, data and computing power, rather than relying on other countries or companies.

Reverse Engineering Rv

Taking a finished product, program or AI model apart to work out how it was made and how it works.

Mechanistic Interpretability Mp

Looking inside an AI model, piece by piece, to find which parts of it store particular ideas and how they lead to its answers.

Activation Steering As

Nudging a model’s internal signals while it responds, to strengthen or weaken a particular behaviour.

Model Extraction Mx

Sending an AI system thousands of questions and using its answers to build a copy, without access to its files. It usually breaks the tool’s terms of use and may break the law.

Refusal Direction Rd

A single pattern in a model’s internal numbers that researchers found is linked to it refusing unsafe requests. Removing this pattern can stop the model refusing, which is how abliteration works.

Abliteration Ab

Editing an AI model to strip out its safety protections, so it no longer refuses harmful requests. Doing this does not make harmful or illegal content legal.

Uncensored Model Uc

A downloadable AI model that people have altered, by retraining or by abliteration, so it no longer refuses harmful requests. Illegal content is still illegal, whichever tool makes it. Contrast with jailbreaking, which tricks an unchanged model.

Jailbreaking Jb

Writing prompts designed to trick an AI into ignoring its safety rules, without changing the model itself. It usually breaks the tool’s terms of use.

GPU Gp

Graphics Processing Unit: a chip first made for video games that can do thousands of calculations at the same time. That makes it the main chip used to train and run AI.

TPU & Accelerators Tp

Computer chips built just for AI. They do AI’s maths faster and use less power than ordinary chips. Google’s TPU (Tensor Processing Unit) is one example.

Cloud AI Cd

AI that runs on powerful computers in big data centres and is used over the internet, rather than on your own device. Most chatbots work this way.

Edge AI Ed

Running AI directly on devices such as phones, cameras or sensors, rather than in the cloud.

MLOps Mo

The work of keeping machine learning models working well once people are using them: updating, checking and fixing them.

LLMOps Lo

The everyday work of running large language model tools well: keeping track of prompts, testing the answers, watching the costs and spotting harmful output.

Vector Database Vd

A database that stores information as lists of numbers (embeddings), so it can quickly find things with a similar meaning. Many chatbots use one to look things up before they answer.

Model Serving Ms

The computers and software that run a trained model and answer requests from many people at once.

Quantisation Qz

Shrinking an AI model by storing its numbers with fewer digits, a bit like rounding 3.14159 to 3.1. The model gets smaller, faster and cheaper to run, but can become slightly less accurate.

Distillation Ds

Training a smaller “student” model to mimic the behaviour of a larger “teacher” model.

Open-Weight Model Ow

A model whose trained parameters are publicly released, so anyone can run, study or adapt it, usually under a licence that sets some rules. Sharing it openly helps research, but also lets people remove its safety features.

Small Language Model Sm

A compact language model designed to run quickly and cheaply, often directly on a device.

Evals Ev

Structured tests that measure a model’s accuracy, safety and behaviour on specific tasks.

Scaling Laws Sl

The pattern researchers have seen that AI models tend to get better, in a predictable way, when they are made bigger and trained on more data with more computing power.

Data Pipeline Dp

The automated flow that collects, cleans and prepares data for training and analysis.
