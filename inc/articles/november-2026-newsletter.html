Oracy is back on the curriculum. The Education Secretary has announced that oracy (public speaking, structured debate and attentive listening) will sit alongside reading and writing in England’s national curriculum, as a new strand of English assessed up to and including GCSE.

  • Spring 2027: the final revised national curriculum is due to be published.
  • September 2028: first teaching of the new curriculum begins, cementing oracy in classrooms across England.

It’s perfect timing for AI Awareness Day 2027. The National AI Conversation, which opens on 1 January, exists to build exactly these skills: young people questioning, discussing and debating the role AI should play in their lives, building to AI Awareness Day on Thursday 29 April 2027.

Why oracy, why now

  • The AI imperative. As generative AI takes over more routine writing and recall, the distinctly human skills (reasoning, debate and structured conversation) are what future-proof students. When a machine can draft the essay, the value shifts to the person who can explain and defend it.
  • Dismantling privilege. Confident speaking has long been a marker of privilege. The reform aims to make structured communication every child’s right, levelling the playing field for further study and careers.
  • Academic and social growth. Education campaigners point to evidence that teaching oracy explicitly raises attainment, builds confidence and supports students’ wellbeing.

Eight skills

AI can answer almost any question in seconds. So what should young people be learning? We think the answer is the skills no machine can practise for them, and oracy comes first:

  1. Oracy & Communication: fluent spoken language, confidence and the ability to articulate complex ideas
  2. Public Speaking: being articulate and exploring technical concepts in front of an audience
  3. Critical Thinking: evaluating arguments logically and spotting cognitive biases
  4. Information Literacy: examining evidence, identifying bias and verifying facts
  5. Conflict Resolution: resolving conflict in pressured situations
  6. Active Listening & Empathy: engaging deeply with other people’s viewpoints and responding respectfully
  7. Independent Judgement: forming convictions backed by reasoning rather than peer pressure
  8. Civil Discourse: adapting tone and voice to defuse tension and solve problems together

A debate to try

If an AI sounded exactly like a person, would you still keep a human in the loop?

Block (Jack Dorsey’s company) has unveiled Dessa, a research voice AI that learned to talk from recordings of real conversation, with no written text at all. Block says it can listen and speak at the same time, laugh mid-sentence and pick up on pauses and sarcasm. These are the company’s own claims; there are no independent tests yet. It makes a perfect classroom debate:

  • Safe: if a voice on the phone sounds human, how would you check?
  • Smart: when should a voice AI act for you, and when should you decide yourself?
  • Responsible: should an AI have to tell you it isn’t human?
  • Future: if AI can chat like us, what conversation skills must stay human?
